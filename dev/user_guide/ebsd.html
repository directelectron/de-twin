EBSD in an SEM#

The twin can be an SEM with a Direct Electron camera as its electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) detector: a bulk polished specimen tilted to 70°, scanned point by point like 4D-STEM, one Kikuchi pattern per scan point. de-twin produces the patterns and the ground truth (phase, grain, orientation, pattern centre per point); index them with the tool of your choice.

from de_twin import DigitalTwin
from de_twin.state import ScanRequest

twin = DigitalTwin("EBSD duplex steel", camera="Celeritas")
twin.configure_ebsd(beam_kv=20.0, sample_tilt_deg=70.0, pcx=0.5, pcy=0.3, pcz=0.6, probe_current_na=0.1)
request = twin.request(
    hw_binning=(4, 4),
    frame_time_s=1e-3,
    total_frames=100 * 80,
    scan=ScanRequest(enabled=True, size=(100, 80), step_um=0.5, dwell_s=1e-3),
)

for raw, meta in twin.frames(request):  # raw camera frames, 256 x 256, as DE-Server sends them
    ...
patterns = twin.ebsd_patterns(request)  # noiseless stack (80, 100, 256, 256), e- / binned px / s
# orix CrystalMap: phase, orientation, grain_id, pcx/pcy/pcz, band_contrast per scan point
# (x, y in um)
xmap = twin.ground_truth(request)

configure_ebsd sets OpticsConfig.ebsd, an EbsdGeometry: a frozen dataclass of plain numbers (to_dict / from_dict / replace, describe() for ranges), so a GUI can persist it. With it set, every acquisition is RenderMode.EBSD; enabled=False returns the twin to a TEM. The beam energy is the column’s high tension (beam_kv; 5–30 kV). The deapi face exposes the geometry as Simulator EBSD ... properties.

The twin’s own column becomes an SEM too, reporting what DE-TEM-Channel’s SEM backends report: InstrumentType is "JEOL SEM (EIK)" ("Thermo Fisher / FEI xT" for an FEI column; de_twin.column.is_sem recognizes both), TemStemMode is 1, and ProjectionMode and SubMode are 0. The column also has two SEM properties, WorkingDistance (mm) and SampleTilt (degrees). Writing either one moves the geometry the patterns are rendered from, and on a TEM both read -1. Over the DE-TEM-Channel face, the defocus slot (getDeFocusValue, setDefocus, PROJ_Defocus) carries the working distance in meters, as on a real SEM. getProperty WorkingDistance / SampleTilt answer in mm and degrees.

The pattern stack is ny * nx * h * w * 4 bytes; pass out=np.memmap(...) (or dtype) to ebsd_patterns for large scans.

A BulkSurface is an infinite polished surface: a Voronoi tessellation of a hashed jittered lattice (deterministic, evaluated point by point), a phase per grain from the phase fractions, and an orientation per grain drawn from the phase’s Texture, unique to the grain. Build your own with BulkSurfaceConfig. A TEM specimen also works in EBSD mode (its field map’s phases and grain orientations, no boundary mixing).

Conventions#

  • Sample frame (orientations are reported in it): x_s = scan x (right), y_s = scan y (down), z_s into the sample. The tilt axis is x_s; the detector is at the top of the SEM image. Orientations are orix rotations: crystal→sample matrix G = R.to_matrix().T, exactly as everywhere else in de-twin (crystal_to_lab).

  • Detector frame: x_d along pattern columns, y_d down the rows, z_d from the source to the screen. The pattern is the screen as seen from the sample: the bright forward-scatter region is at the bottom.

  • Pattern centre: Bruker convention. pcx from the left in units of the sensor width, pcy from the top in units of the sensor height, pcz = detector distance / sensor height, all for the whole unbinned sensor (binning and ROI leave them unchanged). de_twin.ebsd.convert_pc gives the EDAX/TSL, Oxford and EMsoft (v5) forms.

  • The pattern centre follows the beam: EbsdGeometry.pcx/pcy/pcz are for the beam on the optic axis (scan centre) at calibration_wd_mm. A beam offset (dx, dy) on the sample moves the source by S2D @ (dx, dy, 0): pcx += dx/(W p), pcy -= sin(σ−θ) dy/(H p), pcz += cos(σ−θ) dy/(H p) (σ sample tilt, θ detector tilt, p pixel pitch). A working distance change moves it too. The ground truth carries the pattern centre of every point.

  • Scan step is on the tilt-corrected sample surface (ScanRequest.step_um, else EbsdGeometry.step_um).

  • Strain (indented specimens): the deformation gradient F in the sample frame maps the reference lattice to the deformed one (u = F u0); F = R U with strain = U − I and rotation the rotation vector of R (radians). A deformed crystal’s pattern is the reference pattern read along F⁻¹ r: zone axes move with F, plane normals with F⁻ᵀ.

Model#

  • Master patterns: kinematic, one per phase and beam energy, built from the crystal library’s structure factors (Lobato scattering factors, Debye–Waller) and cached process-wide. Every ±g pair gives a band of width 2θ_B and weight |F_g|²; higher orders nest inside. Band edges are blurred by the backscatter energy spread (energy_spread_frac) and the screen’s angular blur, with an excess line inside and a deficiency outside each edge. Standardised and the bright tail log-compressed, they are stored as uint8 on a square-Lambert hemisphere (kinematic patterns are centrosymmetric; this is also the exchange format) and on a gnomonic cube map, which the renderer reads (one reciprocal per lookup instead of a square root, a division and an arctangent): 2.7 MB per phase at 0.15°, ~1 s to build.

  • Projection: per scan point, one threaded numba pass over the binned detector grid composes the grain orientation, the deformation gradient, the sample and detector tilts and the point’s own pattern centre, samples each binned pixel (supersample × supersample points), adds the background and writes the detector’s Poisson means as it goes (Detector.expose(means=)): no full-sensor render and no second pass over the frame.

  • Signal: backscatter yield (Arnal, by Z and tilt) × probe current × an angular distribution (a cosine about the surface normal plus a forward lobe about the specular direction, sharper at higher kV) × each pixel’s solid angle (cos³γ / DD²), shadowed below the sample surface; bands modulate it by 1 + band_contrast × quality × m. The default band_contrast = 0.06 puts strong bands ~9–11 % and zone axes ~15 % above the background in raw patterns, as in real raw EBSD patterns (5–15 %).

  • Grain boundaries: within the interaction footprint (0.02 × the Kanaya–Okayama range across the tilt axis, 1/cos σ longer along it; ~30 × 80 nm for Ni at 20 kV) the neighbouring grain’s pattern is mixed in by the fraction of the footprint across the boundary. That alone touches only scan points within the footprint (a dotted line at 0.5 µm steps), so bulk specimens also have a boundary zone (boundary_zone_um, 0.2 µm σ; quality −50 % on the boundary): grooves, relief and distorted lattice next to a boundary, which is what makes boundaries continuous dark lines in real band-contrast maps. Surface damage and polishing scratches lower quality too.

  • Noise: the camera’s own detector model. At 20 kV an electron stops in the sensor and deposits all its energy (CameraModel.full_stop_kv), ~1600 ADU on a Celeritas: an integrating camera holds about one electron per pixel per frame, so EBSD runs at low current (default 0.1 nA), binned, or summed over frames.

What it does not have, compared with EMsoft dynamical master patterns: no Bloch-wave intensities or channelling (band intensities are kinematic |F|², not dynamical), no Monte-Carlo depth and energy distribution of the backscattered electrons (one effective energy and a fixed edge blur), no HOLZ rings or fine zone-axis structure, no anomalous absorption, and excess / deficiency edges that are symmetric and fixed in the crystal rather than set by the scattering geometry. The background is a smooth heuristic, not a Monte-Carlo result. For dynamical patterns, load your own master (below).

Your own master patterns#

twin.set_master_pattern("Nickel", "Ni-master-20kV.h5")  # EMsoft EMEBSDmaster output
twin.set_master_pattern("Nickel", "ni_mp.hspy", energy_kv=20)  # kikuchipy EBSDMasterPattern.save()
twin.set_master_pattern("Silicon", array, projection="lambert", hemisphere="both", kv=20.0)
twin.master_patterns()  # what is in use, sizes, checks
twin.clear_master_patterns()  # back to kinematic

  • Formats: EMsoft HDF5 (EMData/EBSDmaster/mLPNH / mLPSH, or the stereographic masterSPNH; per-site sets summed; EkeVs), kikuchipy / HyperSpy HDF5 (Experiments/<title>/data with hemisphere and energy axes, projection / hemisphere attributes), or a NumPy array — (n, n), (2, n, n) north/south, (nE, n, n) with energies, (2, nE, n, n) — square Lambert or stereographic.

  • Energy: energy_kv picks (interpolates) an energy bin; otherwise the Monte-Carlo energy-weighted sum when the file has the histogram (EMData/MCOpenCL/accum_e), else the highest energy. The master is used within 0.5 kV of its kv.

  • Hemispheres: a southern hemisphere that is the inversion of the northern one is dropped; otherwise both are kept (non-centrosymmetric phases; six cube faces) and the south is read at Lambert(x, y, |z|), as EMsoft stores it.

  • Checks: the file’s space group must be the phase’s; the pattern must be invariant under the phase’s point group (symmetry_error; a transposed or mirrored projection is refused).

  • Memory: resampled and quantised to EbsdGeometry.master_resolution_deg on load (an EMsoft file is read one energy slice at a time): 2.7 MB centrosymmetric, 5.4 MB with two hemispheres at 0.15°.

  • de_twin.ebsd.io.write_emsoft writes the EMsoft layout (used by the round-trip test: a kinematic master exported and re-imported gives the same patterns, correlation > 0.998).

  • deapi: Simulator EBSD Master Pattern Phase, ... File (a path on the twin’s machine: deapi properties are strings, so there is no upload) and ... Status.

Indents and HR-EBSD#

EBSD Si (001) Vickers indent (and the Berkovich preset; Indent for your own) carries the residual field of an indent (de_twin.specimen.indent): outside the plastic zone a traction-free surface field with a deviatoric part decaying as 1/r² (radial compression, hoop tension, with the indenter’s and the cubic crystal’s four-fold modulation), a Yoffe-blister dilatation decaying as 1/r³, and the surface tilt of the residual uplift (2° at the plastic-zone edge, 1/r²) plus a small twist; a plastic zone (1.8 × the contact size) with smoothly varying random rotations up to 3° and falling pattern quality; the amorphised impression; radial cracks along ⟨110⟩ from the corners (dark lines and a mode-I crack-tip field) and a chipped lateral-crack sector. The strain distorts the patterns (F⁻¹ per pixel direction) and the rotation moves them, so cross-correlation HR-EBSD measures them. ground_truth adds deformation_gradient, strain, rotation and gnd (a GND density proxy: the in-plane Nye tensor norm over b, 1/m²).

The test suite runs a test-only Wilkinson / Britton-Wilkinson HR-EBSD (FFT cross-correlation of 7 × 7 ROIs, remapping, 8-parameter deviatoric fit) against a reference 57 µm away: on full-resolution 1024² Celeritas patterns it recovers the deviatoric strain to 1.3e-4 RMS and the rotation to 0.007° RMS at 4000 e⁻ per pixel of shot noise (4e-5 and 0.002° at 16 000; 3.6e-5 and 0.001° noiseless). With kinematic bands (no dynamical fine structure) that is the dose it takes; below ~1000 e⁻/px the cross-correlation fails.

A scan costs ~80 bytes per point (+72 with strain) plus a copy of its phases’ cube maps (1.8 MB each); streaming 512² frames peaks at ~400–500 MB RSS, mostly the detector’s maps. Master patterns are built on first use of a phase and energy (~1 s) and kept up to 64 MB.

The test suite checks the geometry (zone axes and band edges where the geometry puts them, the pattern centre moving with the beam), round-trips the ground truth, indexes noisy frames with a test-only dictionary + refinement (~0.06° median at ~45 e⁻ per binned pixel), round-trips master patterns through the EMsoft and kikuchipy layouts, and recovers the indent’s strain with HR-EBSD.
